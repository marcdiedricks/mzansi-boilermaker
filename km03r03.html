Computer Numerical Control (CNC) Applications

Understand what CNC means, where it can support fabrication, its limitations, and why controlled specifications and authorised procedures matter.

← KM-03 lessons
Learning only. This lesson explains CNC concepts and safe decision points. It does not teach CNC setup, programming, machine operation, cutting parameters or any other hazardous practical procedure.

1. What does CNC mean?

Computer Numerical Control (CNC) refers to machines whose movements or processes are controlled by programmed numerical information. In fabrication, CNC systems can support repeatable work where approved specifications are converted into controlled machine instructions.

Key idea: CNC does not remove the need for correct drawings, specifications, inspection, authorised procedures or competent supervision.

2. Where CNC can be used

CNC technology can be applied in fabrication environments for controlled cutting, shaping or other machine processes. The exact application depends on the machine, approved job information and workplace process.

This lesson focuses on recognition and understanding, not machine operation.

3. CNC has limitations

A CNC machine can only produce reliable results when the correct information, machine capability, material, tooling, maintenance status and approved process are in place.

Incorrect specifications, unsuitable materials, machine limitations or unapproved changes can lead to incorrect work or unsafe conditions.

4. Controlled specifications matter

The curriculum expects learners to understand that component specifications may be captured into a CNC programme. At learner-companion level, the important point is that specifications must come from approved job information and must be checked through the responsible process.

This app does not provide CNC programming steps or operating instructions.

5. CNC and conventional machines are different

Conventional machines depend more directly on manual machine control, while CNC machines use programmed numerical instructions to control defined movements or processes. Both still require competent people, approved procedures, inspection and maintenance.

6. Stop-and-clarify rule

If the programme, drawing, specification, machine status or instruction is unclear, conflicting or outside your training, stop and clarify through the responsible workplace or training process.
