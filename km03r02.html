KM-03 • LESSON 2 OF 4

Trade Related Machines and Machine Operations

Recognise common stationary machines used in fabrication, their general purpose, key hazard zones, and the need for approved procedures, inspection and competent supervision.

← KM-03 lessons
Learning only. This lesson is for machine recognition and safety awareness. It does not teach machine setup, settings, operation, isolation, maintenance, fault-finding or practical use. Practical work requires approved training, workplace procedures and competent human supervision.

1. What are trade-related stationary machines?

Boilermaker fabrication can involve stationary workshop machines used for controlled cutting, bending, shaping or forming work. Examples named in the curriculum include bending machines, plate rollers, shaping rollers, press brakes, guillotines and cutting machines.

Key idea: Recognising a machine and its purpose is not the same as being trained or authorised to operate it.

2. Match the machine to the approved job requirement

The correct machine depends on the job information, material, dimensions, required result and approved workplace process. Learners should not select or improvise machine use from appearance or memory alone.

3. Machine terminology matters

Workplaces use specific terminology for machines, attachments, guards, controls, work areas and hazards. Understanding the correct terms helps learners communicate clearly and recognise when they need guidance.

4. Recognise hazard zones and safeguards

Stationary machines can contain moving, rotating, cutting, shearing, bending, pressing or trapping hazards. Guards and other safeguards form part of the approved machine safety system.

This lesson does not explain how to bypass, adjust or defeat guards or safety devices.

5. Inspection and maintenance are controlled activities

Machine condition, inspection status and maintenance affect safe use. If a machine appears damaged, has missing safeguards, shows an unclear status or behaves unexpectedly, the learner should stop and use the responsible reporting process.

Inspection and maintenance procedures must be carried out only within the learner’s approved role, training and workplace controls.

6. Safe operating procedures remain the authority

The official curriculum requires knowledge of safe operating procedures for trade-related stationary machines. In this companion, that requirement is handled at recognition level: learners must understand that approved procedures exist, must be followed, and cannot be replaced by a video, memory or informal advice.
