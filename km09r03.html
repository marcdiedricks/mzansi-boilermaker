Active Learning, Ideation and Learning Strategies

Curriculum-aligned learning support for emotional intelligence, reasoning, problem solving and active learning.

Learning support only. This module develops workplace thinking and interpersonal skills. It does not diagnose personality, mental health or competence, and it does not replace facilitator or workplace judgement.

1. What active learning means

Active learning requires the learner to do more than read or listen. It can include recalling information, explaining an idea in one's own words, answering questions, comparing examples and identifying weak areas.

2. What ideation means

Ideation is the process of generating and developing ideas or possible solutions. It helps learners explore more than one way of understanding or responding to a problem before choosing the most suitable option.

3. Stages of ideation

A simple ideation process can include understanding the challenge, generating several possibilities, comparing them against requirements, selecting a promising option and refining it.

4. Generating ideas and solutions

Useful idea-generation activities may include asking questions, brainstorming, sketching concepts, discussing alternatives, comparing examples and testing an idea against known requirements.

5. Learning strategies

Retrieval practice, short quizzes, spaced review, explaining a concept aloud and revisiting weak areas can improve learning. These strategies fit the Mzansi Artisan lesson cycle.

Key idea: Active learning means working with the knowledge, not only looking at it.
