Integrity and Social Responsibility

Curriculum-aligned learning support for decision making, self-governance, integrity and personal responsibility.

← KM-13 lessons
Learning support only. These lessons develop thinking, ethics and workplace judgement. They do not replace workplace authority, legal duties, supervision or formal assessment.

1. Choices and actions

Personal choices affect other people, the workplace and the wider community. Social responsibility means considering those effects when deciding how to act.

2. Integrity

Integrity means acting consistently with accepted ethical principles, even when doing so is inconvenient or not being watched.

3. Integrity in relationships

Trust in teams and workplaces depends on honest communication, accurate records, fair treatment and keeping commitments.

4. Pressures that weaken responsibility

Fear, peer pressure, convenience, personal gain and group habits can make responsible behaviour harder. Recognising those pressures helps a learner respond more deliberately.

Key idea: Integrity matters most when there is pressure to compromise it.
