Pipe and Plate Templates

Recognise how pipe and plate template information represents required shapes and features, and why controlled job information must remain the authority.

← Knowledge Modules
Learning only. This lesson develops recognition and understanding. It does not teach practical marking-out, cutting, heating, forming, welding, machine operation or fabrication. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Why are pipe and plate templates used?

Pipe and plate work can involve shapes, openings, profiles and relationships that must correspond with approved job information. A template can provide a controlled representation of the required feature so that the information can be recognised and interpreted consistently.

Key idea: The template is a reference. The current approved drawing, specification or job information remains the authority.

2. Pipe template information

Pipe-related templates can represent required profiles or relationships associated with a particular component or joint. The learner should be able to recognise that the template must correspond to the correct pipe or component, orientation and controlled job reference.

This knowledge lesson does not provide dimensions, cutting patterns or instructions for making a pipe template.

3. Plate template information

Plate-related templates can represent outlines, profiles, openings or repeated features specified by controlled job information. Their usefulness depends on correct identification and a clear link to the job requirement.

A learner should not assume that a template is suitable merely because its shape appears similar.

4. Identity, orientation and revision

Before relying on template information, recognise the importance of checking identity, intended component or feature, orientation where relevant, and current revision or issue status. These controls help prevent an obsolete or unrelated reference from being mistaken for the required one.

If any controlled information is missing, unclear or conflicting, stop and clarify through the responsible training or workplace process.

5. Relationship to drawings and specifications

Templates do not stand alone. They must remain connected to the approved information that defines the job. A drawing, specification or other authorised reference may contain requirements that cannot be understood from the template shape alone.

6. Recognition before practical work

At knowledge level, the learner should recognise what a pipe or plate template represents, how it is identified, and why its controlled source matters. Manufacturing, transferring or using a template in fabrication belongs to supervised practical training and approved workplace procedures.
