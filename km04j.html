Recognise Fabrication Records and Material Traceability Before Work Begins

A short recognition-and-quality lesson within KM-04 Fabrication and Pipework.

← Main app

Learning only. This lesson explains why material identity and fabrication records must remain traceable. It does not teach marking, cutting, welding, heating, forming, lifting, machine setup, machine operation or any other hazardous practical procedure. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. What does material traceability mean?

Material traceability means being able to connect a physical material or component to the records that identify its origin, grade, batch, heat, lot or other controlled reference where the job requires it.

2. Why identification matters

Fabrication records help confirm that the material and the job information still match. Losing or mixing identification can break the traceability chain and make later quality checks more difficult.

3. Records must stay linked to the job

Job references, approved drawings, material certificates, tags, heat or lot references and inspection records may all form part of a controlled traceability system. The learner's task here is to recognise the purpose of those links, not to create or certify them.

4. If identity is unclear, stop and clarify

If material identification or the related record is missing, unclear or inconsistent, do not guess. Stop and clarify through the responsible training or workplace process before practical work proceeds.
