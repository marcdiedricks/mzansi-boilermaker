Plasma Cutting — Theory

Curriculum-aligned theory support for Cutting and Welding.

← KM-06 lessons
Theory only. Cutting, welding and heat-treatment processes involve serious hazards. This lesson explains concepts, process differences, quality, hazards and decision boundaries only. It does not provide operating sequences, machine settings, live-work instructions or authorisation. Practical training must be conducted in an approved environment under competent human supervision.

1. Plasma cutting concept

Plasma cutting is a thermal cutting process used in appropriate fabrication applications. Learners should recognise its purpose and general characteristics without operating the equipment.

2. Process selection

Material type, thickness, required quality, equipment capability and workplace procedure influence whether plasma cutting is appropriate.

3. Hazards

Plasma cutting can involve high energy, intense heat, radiation, fumes, noise and electrical hazards. Approved controls and trained supervision are essential.

4. Quality awareness

Cut quality is judged against controlled requirements. The learner should understand the role of dimensions, edge condition and inspection without attempting to optimise machine settings.

Key idea: No live operating settings are included.
