Basic Templates

Learn what fabrication templates are, why different template types and materials are used, and how approved job information gives a template its meaning.

← KM-04 lessons
Learning only. This lesson is for recognising and understanding templates. It does not teach practical marking-out, cutting, forming, machine operation or any other hazardous fabrication activity. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. What is a fabrication template?

A fabrication template is a controlled reference used to represent a required shape, outline, position or relationship for a component. Its meaning comes from the approved drawing, specification or other controlled job information.

Key idea: A template supports accurate interpretation. It does not replace the current approved drawing or specification.

2. Why are there different kinds of templates?

Different jobs can require different template forms. Some may represent a full shape, part of a shape, a repeated feature, a profile or a reference used to check relationships between features.

The learner should recognise that the correct template type depends on the controlled job requirement, not on guesswork or convenience.

3. Template materials

Templates may be made from different suitable materials depending on the workplace requirement, durability needed, accuracy expected and how the reference will be controlled or reused.

The correct material and identification method must come from approved workplace or training information. This lesson does not instruct the learner to manufacture a template.

4. What information gives a template meaning?

A template should be linked to the correct job, drawing or specification. Important controlled information can include identity, orientation, dimensions, reference points, revision status and the component or feature represented.

If the identity, revision or purpose is unclear, stop and clarify through the responsible training or workplace process.

5. Basic marking-off principles at knowledge level

Marking-off is the controlled transfer or representation of job information so that positions, outlines or reference features can be understood before fabrication. Accuracy depends on using the correct approved information and recognising the correct reference points.

This lesson explains the principle only. Practical marking-out belongs in supervised practical training.

6. How templates fit into fabrication planning

Templates help connect drawing information to a planned component or feature. Before a template is relied on, the learner should recognise the need to check that it belongs to the correct job, matches the current controlled information and is suitable for the intended purpose.
