Slinging Regular Loads — Principles and Safety Awareness

Curriculum-aligned theory support for basic slinging, lifting and load securing.

← KM-14 lessons
High-risk theory only. Slinging, lifting, crane direction, load movement and load securing can cause severe injury if done incorrectly. This lesson teaches recognition, planning concepts, hazard awareness and decision boundaries only. It does not provide rigging instructions, sling-selection procedures, working-load calculations, hand-signal sequences, operating steps or authorisation for real lifting work. Practical training requires approved equipment, formal workplace procedures and competent adult supervision.

1. What slinging means

Slinging is the controlled preparation of a load so that it can be lifted by approved lifting equipment. At theory level, learners should understand that load identity, weight, shape, condition and approved lifting information matter before any lift is planned.

2. Regular-load concept

The curriculum refers to regular loads up to 3 tons. A regular load is not automatically safe or simple; its characteristics still have to be understood by trained and authorised people using the applicable workplace procedure.

3. Hazard awareness

Suspended loads, shifting loads, damaged equipment, unstable conditions and poor communication can create severe hazards. Learners should recognise these hazard categories and stay outside the lifting zone unless authorised.

4. Human authority

The app cannot determine a real sling arrangement, approve a lifting point or confirm that a load is safe to lift.

Key idea: Recognition is appropriate for the app; rigging decisions belong to competent authorised people.
