Basic Principles of Computers and Computing Systems

Curriculum-aligned learning support for basic computers, communication software, digital responsibility, 4IR and design thinking.

← KM-10 lessons
Learning support only. This module develops basic digital literacy for the occupational environment. Workplace IT policies, privacy rules, cybersecurity controls and authorised software usage remain binding.

1. How computers process information

A computer receives input, processes information using its processor, stores information in memory or storage, and produces output. Understanding these basic functions helps learners use digital tools more confidently.

2. Communication technology

Computers, phones and connected devices can support workplace communication through messages, documents, meetings and information systems.

3. Access to information

Digital systems can provide fast access to learning materials, job information and workplace records. Learners should check whether information is current, authorised and appropriate before relying on it.

4. Cell phones in the workplace

Mobile phones can support communication, photographs, documents and learning, but workplace rules may limit when, where and how they may be used.

5. Social media awareness

Social media can spread information quickly, but workplace information, personal data and confidential material should not be shared without permission.

Key idea: Digital convenience does not remove workplace privacy, confidentiality or information-control duties.
