Gas, Arc, TIG and MIG Welding Processes — Flat Welding Theory

Curriculum-aligned theory support for Cutting and Welding.

← KM-06 lessons
Theory only. Cutting, welding and heat-treatment processes involve serious hazards. This lesson explains concepts, process differences, quality, hazards and decision boundaries only. It does not provide operating sequences, machine settings, live-work instructions or authorisation. Practical training must be conducted in an approved environment under competent human supervision.

1. Process families

Gas welding, arc welding, TIG and MIG are different welding process families. Learners should recognise their names, general purposes and the fact that each process has its own equipment, consumables, controls and suitability.

2. Flat-position concept

A flat welding position describes the orientation of the work and weld in a learning or fabrication context. Position affects technique, access and process control, but practical execution belongs to supervised training.

3. Quality factors

Weld quality depends on suitable materials, joint preparation, procedure, equipment condition, consumables, operator competence and inspection. The PWA must not reduce quality to a single setting or visual cue.

4. Hazards

Welding involves hazards such as intense heat, radiation, fumes, fire, electricity and hot metal. Learners should understand the hazard categories and the need for approved workplace controls.

Key idea: Process recognition is appropriate for the app; live welding instruction is not.
