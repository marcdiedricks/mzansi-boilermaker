Boilermaking-Specific Health, Safety and Environmental Protection Concepts

Recognise the main health, safety and environmental hazards associated with boilermaking and understand when work must stop and be escalated through the approved process.

← KM-02 lessons
Learning only. This lesson is hazard-recognition theory. It does not teach welding, gas-cylinder handling, confined-space entry, work at height, electrical setup, fire-fighting technique or any other hazardous practical procedure. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Boilermaking has trade-specific hazards

Boilermaking work can involve fumes, heavy or hot materials, electrical cables, work at height, poor ground conditions and exposure to heat, cold, wind or rain.

Key idea: Recognising a hazard does not mean the learner is authorised to control it independently. Use the approved workplace process.

2. Protect other people around the work

Boilermaking hazards can affect people who are not doing the task. Nearby workers, visitors and other teams may be exposed to fumes, heat, sparks, noise, moving materials or restricted access areas.

The learner should recognise the need for approved barriers, warnings, work-area controls and supervision where applicable.

3. Confined spaces can become dangerous quickly

Confined spaces can allow pollutants to build up and can develop unsafe atmospheres, including reduced oxygen or displacement of oxygen by gases. Explosion and fire risks may also exist.

A learner must not enter or work in a confined space simply because it appears accessible. Confined-space work requires specific workplace controls, authorisation, monitoring and competent supervision.

4. Compressed gases require controlled handling

Compressed gases can create pressure, fire, explosion, toxicity or oxygen-displacement hazards depending on the gas and the situation.

At this knowledge level, the learner only needs to recognise that gas identification, storage, movement and use must follow the approved workplace system and competent supervision.

5. Electrical and welding equipment need approved safety controls

The curriculum expects awareness of electrical cables and the importance of correct earthing arrangements for welding equipment. Damaged, unclear or uncontrolled electrical arrangements must be reported through the responsible process.

This lesson does not give setup, connection or operating instructions for welding equipment.

6. Fire risk and emergency readiness

Hot work and fabrication environments can involve ignition sources and combustible materials. Learners should recognise the need for approved fire prevention controls, suitable emergency arrangements and trained personnel.

If a fire or emergency condition develops, follow the site emergency procedure and responsible human direction. This lesson is not fire-fighting training.
