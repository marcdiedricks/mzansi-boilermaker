Basics of 4IR and Future Skills

Curriculum-aligned learning support for basic computers, communication software, digital responsibility, 4IR and design thinking.

← KM-10 lessons
Learning support only. This module develops basic digital literacy for the occupational environment. Workplace IT policies, privacy rules, cybersecurity controls and authorised software usage remain binding.

1. What 4IR means

The Fourth Industrial Revolution describes the growing use and connection of digital technologies such as automation, connected systems, data, artificial intelligence and advanced manufacturing.

2. Emerging trends

Occupational environments increasingly use digital records, automated equipment, sensors, software-assisted design and data-supported decision-making.

3. Future skills

Useful future skills include digital literacy, problem solving, communication, adaptability, safe technology use and continuous learning.

4. Employability

Understanding new technologies can improve a learner's ability to adapt to changing workplaces, but technology knowledge should be combined with trade knowledge, safety and human judgement.

Key idea: New technology changes tools and workflows; it does not remove the need for sound trade fundamentals.
