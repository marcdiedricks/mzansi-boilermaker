Introduction to the Boilermaker Trade

A short starting lesson for a learner entering the current South African Boilermaker qualification.

← Main app
Learning only. This lesson explains the trade. It does not authorise practical work or prove occupational competence.

1. What does a Boilermaker do?

A Boilermaker works with metal components and structures. The trade includes reading technical information, planning work, preparing and forming components, joining and assembling components, and checking work against drawings, specifications and workplace requirements.

Key idea: Good Boilermaker work starts with understanding the job before physical work begins.

2. The qualification has three parts

Knowledge
What you need to understand.
Practical skills
What you learn to do in an appropriate training environment.
Work experience
What you learn through supervised workplace processes.

Completing an app lesson records learning progress only. It does not replace practical training, workplace experience or formal assessment.

3. Work from controlled information

Boilermaker work depends on drawings, measurements, specifications, materials, quality requirements and safe workplace procedures.

If information is unclear, do not guess. Confirm the requirement through the approved training or workplace process.

4. Safety is part of the trade

Some Boilermaker activities can involve significant hazards. Learning the theory is different from being authorised to perform practical work.

Higher-risk practical activities require the applicable workplace controls and competent human supervision.

5. Your learning path

This app groups the formal curriculum into simple learner-facing paths:

READ IT & PLAN IT
Understand drawings, materials, information and planning.
MAKE IT
Learn fabrication concepts and related processes.
FORM IT
Learn how metal components are shaped and prepared.
ASSEMBLE IT
Learn how components and structures come together.
