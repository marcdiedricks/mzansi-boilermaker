Boilermaker Trade Related Tools and Equipment

Recognise the main groups of trade tools and equipment, what they are for, why condition and maintenance matter, and when to stop and ask for guidance.

← KM-03 lessons
Learning only. This lesson develops recognition and safe decision-making. It does not teach the operation of powered tools, machines or hazardous equipment and does not authorise practical work.

1. Tools and equipment must match the job

Boilermaker work uses different hand tools, measuring and layout equipment, power tools and supporting equipment. The correct item depends on the approved job information and workplace process.

Key idea: Recognition comes before use. Do not choose an unfamiliar item by guesswork.

2. Hand tools and equipment

Hand tools are manually operated items used for controlled workshop tasks. At knowledge level, the learner should be able to recognise common categories and understand that each tool has an intended application.

Using a tool for an unintended purpose can damage the tool, the workpiece or create a hazard.

3. Power tools and attachments

Powered tools and attachments can increase the level of risk because they introduce moving parts, energy, noise, vibration and other hazards. The learner should recognise that training, correct selection, inspection status and workplace controls matter before any practical use.

This lesson does not provide operating instructions.

4. Condition and maintenance matter

Tools and equipment must be maintained and kept in a condition suitable for their intended purpose. Damage, missing parts, unclear inspection status or incorrect attachments are reasons to stop and use the responsible reporting process.

5. Storage, identification and control

Correct identification and controlled storage help reduce loss, damage, misuse and confusion. Learners should recognise labels, tool-control systems and workplace rules as part of responsible tool management.

6. Stop-and-ask rule

If the item is unfamiliar, damaged, incorrectly labelled, unsuitable for the task or outside your training, stop and ask the responsible supervisor or training professional.
