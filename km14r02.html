Manual Lifting Equipment and Tackle — Recognition and Control

Curriculum-aligned theory support for basic slinging, lifting and load securing.

← KM-14 lessons
High-risk theory only. Slinging, lifting, crane direction, load movement and load securing can cause severe injury if done incorrectly. This lesson teaches recognition, planning concepts, hazard awareness and decision boundaries only. It does not provide rigging instructions, sling-selection procedures, working-load calculations, hand-signal sequences, operating steps or authorisation for real lifting work. Practical training requires approved equipment, formal workplace procedures and competent adult supervision.

1. Equipment categories

Manual lifting equipment and tackle include specialised devices used to move or support loads. Learners should be able to recognise broad equipment categories and understand that each has a specific purpose, rating and inspection requirement.

2. Identification and status

Labels, certificates, inspection status and manufacturer information form part of equipment control. Unidentified, damaged or out-of-status equipment must not be assumed suitable.

3. Compatibility

Equipment used together must be compatible with the approved lifting plan and workplace procedure. Similar appearance does not prove interchangeability.

4. Inspection boundary

Learners may study what inspection systems are for, but formal inspection and acceptance of lifting equipment belong to authorised competent persons.

Key idea: If equipment identity or status is unclear, stop and escalate.
