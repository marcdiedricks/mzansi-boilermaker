Gas, Arc, TIG and MIG Welding — Down-Hand Position Theory

Curriculum-aligned theory support for Cutting and Welding.

← KM-06 lessons
Theory only. Cutting, welding and heat-treatment processes involve serious hazards. This lesson explains concepts, process differences, quality, hazards and decision boundaries only. It does not provide operating sequences, machine settings, live-work instructions or authorisation. Practical training must be conducted in an approved environment under competent human supervision.

1. Position terminology

The down-hand position is a recognised welding-position concept. Learners should know the term and understand how work orientation influences access, control and quality considerations.

2. Process comparison

Different welding processes can be used for different materials, joints and workplace conditions. The learner should compare process characteristics at a theory level rather than assume one process is suitable for every job.

3. Procedure and specification control

The approved welding procedure, material specification and drawing requirements determine the controlled approach to a job. The PWA does not supply production parameters.

4. Inspection awareness

Welds may be assessed using specified inspection and testing methods. Learners should understand that acceptance criteria come from controlled standards, specifications and quality plans.

Key idea: A visual impression alone is not formal acceptance.
