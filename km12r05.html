Ethics in the Workplace

Curriculum-aligned learning support for workplace relationships and performance.

← KM-12 lessons
Learning support only. This module develops workplace judgement, communication and performance awareness. It does not diagnose health, personality or competence, and it does not replace workplace supervision, HR processes or professional support.

1. Ethical management

Ethical management means making decisions and setting expectations in ways that are honest, fair, responsible and consistent with applicable rules.

2. Common ethical issues

Workplace ethical issues can include harassment, discrimination, health and safety, confidentiality, accurate records, conflicts of interest and misuse of resources.

3. Ethical decision-making

A useful ethical decision considers the facts, who may be affected, applicable rules, possible harm and whether the action can be defended openly.

4. Unethical behaviour

Examples include hiding defects, falsifying records, unfair treatment, sharing confidential information without authority or knowingly ignoring required controls.

5. Consequences

Unethical behaviour can harm people, damage trust, create legal or disciplinary consequences and weaken safety and quality systems.

Key idea: If you would need to hide the action to make it acceptable, that is a warning sign to stop and reassess.
