Introduction to Fabrication and Pipework

A short orientation lesson for KM-04, Fabrication and Pipework, in the current South African Boilermaker curriculum.

← Main app
Learning only. This lesson explains concepts, planning and recognition. It does not teach cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation or any other hazardous practical procedure. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. What does fabrication mean?

Fabrication is the process of making metal components or structures from controlled job information such as drawings, dimensions, specifications and material requirements.

Key idea: Fabrication starts with understanding what must be made before any physical work begins.

2. What does pipework mean?

Pipework refers to pipe components and assemblies that must fit a defined purpose, arrangement and specification. A learner must understand the drawing, pipe information and required dimensions before practical fabrication is attempted.

This lesson deals with recognition and planning only.

3. The drawing controls the job

Drawings and sketches communicate shape, size, position and relationship between components. Specifications and approved job information add further requirements such as material, tolerances and quality expectations.

If controlled information is missing or unclear, do not guess. Use the approved training or workplace process to clarify it.

4. Materials and components must match the requirement

Fabrication and pipework can involve plate, structural sections, pipe and other metal components. Correct identification matters because different jobs require different dimensions, shapes and specifications.

Appearance alone is not enough to confirm material identity or suitability.

5. Accuracy and quality begin in planning

Good fabrication depends on checking the required dimensions, component relationships and quality requirements before practical work starts.

A mistake in understanding a drawing or specification can affect the whole component or assembly.

6. KM-04 is larger than one lesson

The official KM-04 module covers several knowledge areas, including templates, forming and shaping, structural steel fabrication and pipework topics.

This KM-04A lesson is only the orientation. Future slices will unpack those topics one small learning unit at a time.

7. Stop-and-ask rule

Stop and ask a responsible supervisor or training professional when a drawing, material, dimension, component or work instruction is unclear.

Knowing when to stop and clarify information is part of safe, accurate learning.
