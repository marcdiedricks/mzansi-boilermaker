Time Management

Curriculum-aligned learning support for workplace relationships and performance.

← KM-12 lessons
Learning support only. This module develops workplace judgement, communication and performance awareness. It does not diagnose health, personality or competence, and it does not replace workplace supervision, HR processes or professional support.

1. Purpose of time management

Time management helps a person organise priorities, deadlines and available time so that important work is completed reliably.

2. Important versus urgent

A useful planning approach is to distinguish work that is important from work that is merely urgent. This helps prevent constant crisis-driven working.

3. Poor time management

Common problems include unclear priorities, unnecessary interruptions, unrealistic planning, procrastination and failing to allow enough time for required checks.

4. Managing a diary

A paper or electronic diary can help track appointments, deadlines, learning activities and follow-up actions.

5. Safety and quality first

Time pressure must not be used as a reason to skip required safety, inspection or quality steps.

Key idea: Plan time around the required standard; do not lower the standard to fit the clock.
