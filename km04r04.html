Forming and Shaping Simple Components

Recognise the terminology, component types, drawing information, materials and hazards associated with forming and shaping simple components.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson explains concepts, terminology, drawings, hazards and controlled information. It does not provide step-by-step instructions for cutting, welding, heating, bending, lifting, machine operation or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training and competent human supervision.

1. Terminology and controlled meaning

Forming and shaping describe changes made to material so that a component meets the geometry defined by approved drawings and specifications. Terms, codes and symbols must be interpreted from controlled sources rather than guessed.

2. Simple components and steel shapes

Simple fabrication can involve plate, pipe and structural members. A learner should recognise common component categories and understand that material form, section and specification affect suitability.

3. Drawings, symbols and abbreviations

The curriculum expects learners to interpret information used in forming and shaping. At knowledge level, focus on what the symbols, abbreviations and dimensions communicate and when clarification is required.

4. Hazard awareness

Forming and shaping can involve stored energy, heavy material, powered machinery, hot surfaces and pinch or crush hazards. The learning companion therefore limits this lesson to recognition and safety awareness.

5. Standards and specifications

Codes, standards and workplace specifications may control material, tolerances and quality. The applicable reference depends on the actual job and must be verified before use.
