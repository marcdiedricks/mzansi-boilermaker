Occupational Health, Safety and Environment Foundations

Understand the basic safety and environmental responsibilities that form part of learning and working in the Boilermaker trade.

← Knowledge Modules
Learning only. This lesson builds safety awareness. It does not authorise hazardous practical work, replace workplace procedures or teach the operation of tools, machines, compressed-gas systems, welding equipment or other hazardous equipment. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. What does occupational health, safety and environment mean?

Occupational health and safety is about protecting people from harm connected with work. Environmental protection is about preventing avoidable harm to the surroundings through responsible work practices.

In the Boilermaker trade, both are part of doing the job properly. Safety is not an extra task added after the work has been planned.

2. Recognise hazards before work begins

A hazard is something with the potential to cause harm. Risk is connected with the chance and possible seriousness of that harm in a particular situation.

At learner level, the important habit is to look for hazards, use the approved workplace process and ask for clarification when the situation is uncertain.

Key idea: Do not guess your way through an unsafe or unclear situation.

3. Safety is a shared responsibility

Employers, supervisors, trainers and workers all have responsibilities within workplace safety systems. A learner also has responsibilities: follow approved instructions, use required protective measures, report concerns and do not perform work for which you are not trained or authorised.

A learner should also understand that raising a genuine safety concern is part of responsible work behaviour.

4. Protective equipment supports wider controls

Personal protective equipment can be an important part of workplace protection, but it does not make an uncontrolled task automatically safe. The correct protective measures depend on the job, the hazard and the approved workplace requirements.

This lesson does not instruct the learner to select or use specialised protective equipment for hazardous tasks without workplace guidance.

5. Protect other people and the environment

Boilermaker work can affect people nearby and the surrounding work area. Good safety awareness includes recognising the need to protect co-workers, visitors and other workers who may be exposed to a hazard.

Environmental awareness includes following approved controls for waste, spills, emissions, housekeeping and the responsible use of materials and resources.

6. Stop, report and clarify when necessary

If a learner sees an unsafe condition, conflicting instruction, damaged control, unclear requirement or situation outside their training, the correct response is not to improvise. Use the responsible training or workplace reporting process and obtain clarification before continuing.
