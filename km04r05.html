Fabrication Using Structural Steel Shapes

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for the South African National Occupational Certificate: Boilermaker.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports recognition, terminology, drawings, specifications, hazards and quality awareness. It does not teach or authorise cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation, lifting or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Structural steel terminology

Structural steel shapes are identified through recognised names, section information, drawings and specifications. Learners should understand the vocabulary used to describe components without treating similar-looking sections as interchangeable.

2. Characteristics and limitations

Different structural sections are selected for different design purposes. Their dimensions, orientation, material grade and project specification affect suitability and must be confirmed from controlled information.

Key idea: Selection is an engineering and job-information decision, not a visual guess.

3. Codes, standards and specifications

Fabrication quality may depend on project specifications, drawings, applicable standards and workplace quality plans. The correct controlled edition and project requirement must be verified before any compliance claim is made.

4. Hazard awareness

Structural fabrication can involve heavy sections, sharp edges, machinery, hot work, falling objects and suspended loads. Knowledge learning should identify these hazards and reinforce the requirement for approved workplace controls.

5. Quality and traceability

Material identification, revision status, dimensions, inspection requirements and records help preserve traceability. Learners should understand why uncontrolled substitutions or undocumented changes can create quality and safety problems.
