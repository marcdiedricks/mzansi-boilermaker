Fabrication Using Structural Steel Shapes

Recognise structural steel shapes, their limitations, drawing information, fabrication terminology and the hazards that must be controlled in supervised practical work.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson explains concepts, terminology, drawings, hazards and controlled information. It does not provide step-by-step instructions for cutting, welding, heating, bending, lifting, machine operation or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training and competent human supervision.

1. Structural steel shapes

Structural members are manufactured in standard forms for particular engineering uses. The learner should be able to identify broad categories and recognise that dimensions and specifications matter.

2. Characteristics and limitations

Different shapes resist loads and connect to other components in different ways. The correct choice comes from approved design and job information, not from appearance or convenience.

3. Fabrication terminology

Terms, codes and symbols communicate how components are represented and controlled. Formal meanings should be tied to the current drawing, specification and applicable standard.

4. Drawing interpretation

A fabrication drawing can communicate member identity, position, dimensions, connections, holes, notes and revision information. Unclear or conflicting information must be escalated rather than invented.

5. Hazard awareness

Structural fabrication may involve heavy material, powered equipment, sharp edges, hot work and suspended loads. This lesson covers hazard recognition only and does not teach operational procedures.
