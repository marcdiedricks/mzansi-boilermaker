Distortions

Curriculum-aligned theory support for Cutting and Welding.

← KM-06 lessons
Theory only. Cutting, welding and heat-treatment processes involve serious hazards. This lesson explains concepts, process differences, quality, hazards and decision boundaries only. It does not provide operating sequences, machine settings, live-work instructions or authorisation. Practical training must be conducted in an approved environment under competent human supervision.

1. What distortion is

Distortion is an unwanted change in shape or alignment that can occur when material is heated, cut, joined or otherwise processed. Learners should recognise common causes in principle and understand why planning and inspection matter.

2. Why distortion matters

Distortion can affect dimensions, fit-up, alignment and final quality. It may cause a component to fall outside drawing or specification requirements.

3. Control as a planning concept

Distortion control is addressed through approved fabrication planning, sequence, fixturing, procedure and inspection. This lesson does not provide a practical distortion-control recipe.

4. Inspection and escalation

If a component appears distorted or out of tolerance, the correct response is to compare it with controlled requirements and refer the issue through the quality or supervisory process.

Key idea: Do not improvise corrective heating or force a component into place.
