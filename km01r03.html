Quality Control and Quality Assurance Awareness

Understand why quality requirements, inspection, testing, standards, finishing and non-conformances matter in Boilermaker work.

← KM-01 lessons
Learning only. This lesson develops quality awareness. It does not authorise practical inspection, testing, fabrication, machine use or acceptance/rejection decisions reserved for competent workplace personnel.

1. Quality assurance and quality control

Quality assurance is the organised system used to help ensure that work is planned and carried out to the required standard. Quality control focuses on checking work, materials or results against approved requirements.

Key idea: Quality is not based on appearance or opinion alone. It must be checked against controlled requirements.

2. Why inspection and testing matter

Inspection and testing help determine whether work or material meets the required criteria. Different methods have different purposes, limits and applications. A learner should recognise that the correct method comes from the approved quality plan, procedure, drawing, specification or other controlled information.

3. Standards and controlled requirements

Codes, standards, drawings, specifications and workplace procedures can define quality requirements. The learner should recognise the need to use the current approved requirement and not rely on memory, an old revision or an unrelated standard.

This companion does not reproduce copyrighted technical standards or claim that an in-app lesson replaces access to the applicable controlled standard.

4. Finishing preparation

Finishing preparation affects whether completed work can be checked and accepted against the required quality criteria. At knowledge level, the learner should understand that finishing requirements are part of the job information and must be confirmed from the approved process.

5. Non-conformance

A non-conformance is a condition where a product, material, process or result does not meet a specified requirement. Learners should recognise that a suspected non-conformance must be reported and handled through the responsible quality or workplace process rather than hidden, ignored or altered without authorisation.

6. Instruments, gauges and quality problems

Quality processes can involve instruments and gauges used by trained people to check specified characteristics. The important knowledge-level principle is that the correct instrument, method, acceptance criterion and authority must come from the controlled quality process.
