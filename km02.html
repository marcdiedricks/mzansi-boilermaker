Environmental Protection, Health and Safety

A short hazard-awareness lesson based on the current South African Boilermaker curriculum.

← Main app
Learning only. This lesson teaches hazard awareness and safe decision-making. It does not authorise practical work, replace workplace procedures, or prove occupational competence.

1. Safety starts before the task

Safe work begins by understanding the job, the work area, the hazards and the approved workplace controls before practical work starts.

Key idea: If a hazard or instruction is unclear, stop and get guidance from the responsible supervisor or training professional.

2. Recognise hazards

Boilermaking environments can include heat, heavy materials, fumes, electrical hazards, poor ground conditions, weather exposure, work at height and restricted spaces.

The learner’s first responsibility is to recognise that these hazards require the correct workplace controls and competent human supervision.

3. Protect people around the work

Safety is not only about the person doing the task. Other workers and people nearby may also be exposed to hazards.

Work areas, warning signs, barriers and access controls must follow the approved workplace process.

4. PPE is one part of safety

Personal protective equipment may be required for particular tasks, but PPE does not remove the hazard itself.

Correct PPE selection and use must follow the workplace risk assessment, task requirements, manufacturer information and supervisor instruction.

5. Protect the environment

Good boilermaking practice also includes preventing pollution, controlling waste and keeping the work area organised so that people and the environment are protected.

Environmental rules and disposal methods must follow the site’s approved procedures and applicable legal requirements.

6. High-risk conditions need escalation

Confined spaces, compressed gases, welding hazards, fire risks and other high-risk conditions require specific controls, training and supervision.

This app does not provide operational permission or step-by-step instructions for those activities. Use the approved workplace or training process.
