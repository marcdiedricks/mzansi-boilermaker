Emotional Intelligence in Life and Work Situations

Learning support only. This module develops workplace thinking and interpersonal skills. It does not diagnose personality, mental health or competence, and it does not replace facilitator or workplace judgement.

1. What emotional intelligence means

Emotional intelligence is the ability to recognise and manage one's own responses while also noticing and responding appropriately to other people in social and work situations.

2. Self-awareness

Self-awareness means recognising your own emotions, reactions, strengths and limitations. In the workplace, this can help a learner notice when frustration, pressure or uncertainty may affect communication or judgement.

3. Social awareness

Social awareness involves noticing how other people may be experiencing a situation and responding with respect. This supports clearer communication and reduces unnecessary conflict.

4. Responding constructively

An emotionally intelligent response usually involves pausing, considering the situation, communicating respectfully and choosing a response that supports safety, teamwork and the task at hand.

Key idea: Emotional intelligence is about managing responses thoughtfully, not pretending emotions do not exist.

5. Interpersonal and intrapersonal impact

How a learner manages personal reactions can affect relationships with colleagues, supervisors and customers. It can also affect concentration, learning and decision-making.
