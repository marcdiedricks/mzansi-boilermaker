Freehand Sketches and Basic Structural Drawings

Curriculum-aligned theory support for Boilermaker’s Drawings.

← KM-07 lessons
Drawing and theory learning only. This lesson develops interpretation, geometry, measurement and drawing literacy. It does not authorise fabrication, cutting, welding, lifting, machining or any other hazardous practical work.

1. Purpose of freehand sketches

A freehand sketch can communicate an idea or observation quickly, but it must be clearly distinguished from an approved production drawing.

2. Isometric representation

Isometric sketches can help visualise three-dimensional relationships on a two-dimensional page.

3. Orthographic and hidden detail

Basic structural drawings may use orthographic views, hidden detail and sectional views to reveal information that is not visible from the outside.

4. Sectional views

A sectional view represents internal or cut-through information to improve understanding of a component or assembly.

5. Approval boundary

A learner sketch can support communication and learning, but it does not become an authorised fabrication drawing unless accepted through the appropriate engineering or workplace control process.

Key idea: Sketches explain; approved drawings control.
