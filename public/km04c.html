Identify Materials and Components Before Fabrication

A short recognition-and-verification lesson within KM-04 Fabrication and Pipework.

← Main app
Learning only. This lesson supports recognition and checking of materials against controlled job information. It does not teach cutting, welding, heating, bending, lifting, machine setup or machine operation. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Match the material to the job information

A fabrication job may call for a particular material, section, size, thickness, grade or component. The learner should use the approved drawing, specification, material list, identification marks and workplace documentation to confirm what is required.

Key idea: A material that looks similar is not automatically the correct material.

2. Recognise common forms

Boilermaker work can involve plate, flat material, angle sections, channels, beams, hollow sections, pipe and other formed components. Recognition starts with shape and job information, but final suitability must be confirmed from controlled information rather than appearance alone.

3. Size, thickness and grade matter

Two pieces can have a similar shape but different dimensions, thicknesses or material grades. These differences can matter to the finished job. Learners should therefore check the specified identifiers and dimensions before material is accepted for the job.

4. Keep material identity traceable

Workplace systems may use tags, markings, certificates, material lists or other records to keep material identity traceable. If an identification mark is missing, damaged or unclear, do not guess. Stop and ask the responsible supervisor or training professional.

5. Check condition before use

Material condition can affect whether it is suitable for a job. Obvious damage, heavy contamination, unexpected deformation or unclear identification should be reported and checked through the workplace process before practical work continues.
