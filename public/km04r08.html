Pipe Work Characteristics

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for the South African National Occupational Certificate: Boilermaker.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports recognition, terminology, drawings, specifications, hazards and quality awareness. It does not teach or authorise cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation, lifting or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Pipe and flange specifications

Pipework is described using controlled specifications that can include material, size, pressure/service requirements, flange information and other project-specific details. Learners should understand that these values must be read from the applicable approved source rather than guessed.

2. Pipe components

Systems may include straight pipe, fittings, flanges and other components. Their roles and compatibility depend on the system design and specification, so similar appearance does not prove interchangeability.

Key idea: Recognition is useful; selection and approval belong to the competent design/workplace process.

3. Colour coding and identification

Colour coding may be used in some workplaces or standards to support identification, but colour alone must not be treated as proof of contents, pressure, condition or safety. Always follow the applicable site identification and verification system.

4. Controlled standards

Where a technical standard applies, the correct current edition and project scope must be confirmed. This companion should not invent clause numbers or reproduce controlled standards.

5. Safety boundary

Learners must not infer that a line is safe to open, disconnect or work on from labels or colour alone. Real pipe systems require authorised isolation, verification and competent supervision.
