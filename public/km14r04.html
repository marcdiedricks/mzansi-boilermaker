Directing Crane Operations — Communication and Boundaries

Curriculum-aligned theory support for basic slinging, lifting and load securing.

← KM-14 lessons
High-risk theory only. Slinging, lifting, crane direction, load movement and load securing can cause severe injury if done incorrectly. This lesson teaches recognition, planning concepts, hazard awareness and decision boundaries only. It does not provide rigging instructions, sling-selection procedures, working-load calculations, hand-signal sequences, operating steps or authorisation for real lifting work. Practical training requires approved equipment, formal workplace procedures and competent adult supervision.

1. Purpose of direction

Crane operations depend on clear communication between authorised people. The purpose is to coordinate movement safely and prevent conflicting instructions.

2. Standardised communication

Workplaces use approved communication systems, which may include recognised hand signals or radio communication. Learners may study why standardisation matters, but this lesson does not teach operational signal sequences.

3. Visibility and clarity

Poor visibility, unclear communication or conflicting instructions increase risk. The operation should be controlled according to the site's approved procedure.

4. Authority boundary

Directing a real crane operation requires training, authorisation and workplace supervision. A quiz score or app lesson cannot confer that authority.

Key idea: Communication in lifting work must be controlled, unambiguous and authorised.
