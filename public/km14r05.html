Securing Loads for Transportation — Theory and Safety Awareness

Curriculum-aligned theory support for basic slinging, lifting and load securing.

← KM-14 lessons
High-risk theory only. Slinging, lifting, crane direction, load movement and load securing can cause severe injury if done incorrectly. This lesson teaches recognition, planning concepts, hazard awareness and decision boundaries only. It does not provide rigging instructions, sling-selection procedures, working-load calculations, hand-signal sequences, operating steps or authorisation for real lifting work. Practical training requires approved equipment, formal workplace procedures and competent adult supervision.

1. Purpose of load securing

Load securing aims to prevent movement, shifting or loss of a load during transportation. The required method depends on the load, vehicle, route, applicable law and approved workplace procedure.

2. Load characteristics

Shape, mass, centre of gravity, packaging, support and the transport environment influence how a load must be controlled.

3. Inspection and verification

A secured load must be checked through the appropriate workplace process before transport. The app cannot approve a real load-securing arrangement.

4. Hazards in transport

Unsecured or poorly secured loads can shift, fall or affect vehicle stability. Learners should recognise these risks and defer real securing decisions to trained authorised people.

Key idea: A secure-looking load is not the same as a verified safe load.
