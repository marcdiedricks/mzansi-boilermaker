Factors and Limitations Affecting Human Performance

Curriculum-aligned learning support for workplace relationships and performance.

← KM-12 lessons
Learning support only. This module develops workplace judgement, communication and performance awareness. It does not diagnose health, personality or competence, and it does not replace workplace supervision, HR processes or professional support.

1. Human factors

Human performance is influenced by attention, communication, physical condition, task design, the work environment and organisational conditions. Understanding these factors helps explain why good systems do not rely on people being perfect.

2. Performance limits

People have limits in memory, attention, perception and sustained concentration. Fatigue, stress, time pressure, poor conditions and unclear information can reduce performance.

3. Leadership and teamwork

Clear leadership, cooperation and respectful communication help teams coordinate work and notice problems earlier.

4. Physical environment and tasks

Noise, lighting, temperature, vibration, repetitive tasks and poor task design can affect concentration and performance. Workplaces use controls and procedures to reduce these effects.

5. Human error and hazards

Errors can occur when information is missed, tasks are confusing or conditions are difficult. The useful response is to identify contributing factors, report them and improve the system rather than simply blame a person.

Key idea: Good safety systems expect human limitations and build checks around them.
