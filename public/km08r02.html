Basics of Energy Efficiency

Curriculum-aligned learning support for environmental awareness, energy efficiency and ethics.

← KM-08 lessons
Learning support only. This lesson develops workplace awareness and responsible decision-making. It does not replace employer policies, environmental procedures, legislation, supervision or formal assessment.

1. Energy efficiency guidelines and policies

Energy efficiency means achieving the required work while avoiding unnecessary energy use. Workplace policies and applicable rules can guide how equipment, lighting, ventilation and other energy-using systems are managed.

2. Using resources efficiently

Efficient resource use includes avoiding unnecessary waste of electricity, water, consumables and materials while still meeting quality, safety and production requirements.

3. Waste-management relationship

Energy and resource efficiency connect with waste management because wasted material, rework and poor housekeeping can increase environmental impact and operating cost.

4. Improving the environmental footprint

Simple workplace behaviours such as switching off unused equipment where permitted, preventing leaks, avoiding unnecessary rework and following maintenance/reporting procedures can support better resource use.

Key idea: Efficiency must never override safety, quality or authorised operating requirements.
