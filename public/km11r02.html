Fire Causes, Prevention and Emergency Awareness

Curriculum-aligned theory support for workplace fundamentals and workplace safety.

← KM-11 lessons
Safety-awareness learning only. This lesson does not provide emergency-response certification, first-aid certification, fire-fighting authorisation or permission to secure a hazardous worksite. In a real emergency, follow site procedures, alert trained adults/supervisors and use authorised emergency services.

1. How workplace fires can start

Workplace fires can result from electrical faults, uncontrolled ignition sources, flammable materials, gas leaks and poor housekeeping. Learners should be able to recognise these broad hazard categories.

2. Fire-triangle concept

A fire needs fuel, oxygen and an ignition source. This concept helps explain why prevention focuses on controlling combustible material, ignition sources and unsafe conditions.

3. Different fire hazards

Different materials and equipment create different fire risks. The correct response and equipment depend on the site, hazard and trained emergency plan, so learners must not improvise.

4. Emergency response boundary

If a fire or explosion risk is present, learners should raise the alarm and follow the site's emergency and evacuation procedures. Fire-fighting actions belong to trained, authorised responders where the site procedure allows.

Key idea: Recognition and rapid escalation are safer than untrained intervention.
