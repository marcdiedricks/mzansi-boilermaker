General Health, Safety and Environmental Protection Concepts

Understand the general safety, hazard, worksite, PPE, signage and environmental principles that apply before boilermaking-specific hazards are considered.

← KM-02 lessons
Learning only. This lesson supports hazard recognition and responsible decision-making. It does not authorise hazardous practical work or replace workplace risk assessments, procedures, supervision or formal training.

1. Health and safety law provides the framework

South African workplace health and safety requirements create responsibilities for employers and workers. A learner does not need to memorise every legal clause here, but must recognise that workplace safety rules and controls are not optional.

2. General workshop safety

Good workshop safety starts with following approved rules, keeping access routes and work areas controlled, reporting unsafe conditions, and avoiding improvisation when instructions are unclear.

Key idea: Safe work depends on the job, the workplace and the approved controls, not on guesswork.

3. Hazard identification and risk awareness

A hazard is something with the potential to cause harm. Risk considers how likely harm may be and how serious it could be. Learners should recognise hazards and use the responsible workplace process to report, assess or clarify them.

4. Safety signs and colour coding

Safety signs and colours communicate warnings, prohibitions, mandatory actions, emergency information and other controlled messages. The learner must follow the meaning used at the workplace and ask if a sign or instruction is unclear.

5. Personal protective equipment

PPE can form one part of a wider safety system. The correct PPE depends on the hazard, task and approved workplace requirements. PPE does not make an uncontrolled task automatically safe.

6. Securing the worksite

Work areas may need controlled access, barriers, signs or other site controls to protect workers and people nearby. Learners should recognise that these controls must follow the approved workplace process.

7. Environmental protection

Responsible work includes preventing avoidable pollution, controlling waste and spills, protecting drains and surroundings, and following approved disposal and housekeeping procedures.
