Simple Pipe Work Component Fabrication

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for the South African National Occupational Certificate: Boilermaker.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports recognition, terminology, drawings, specifications, hazards and quality awareness. It does not teach or authorise cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation, lifting or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Pipework components

Pipework systems can contain different component types and interfaces. At knowledge level, learners should recognise common categories and understand that component identity, service, material and specification matter.

2. Terminology, codes and symbols

Drawings and specifications use controlled terminology, symbols and abbreviations to describe pipework. Learners should interpret these references without assuming that a familiar symbol or colour has the same meaning in every context.

3. Drawings and specifications

Pipework fabrication knowledge depends on reading the approved drawing and specification, including dimensions and component relationships. Any missing or conflicting information must be escalated for clarification.

Key idea: The drawing and specification control the learning context; the app does not authorise fabrication.

4. Hazards and risks

Pipework work can involve stored pressure, hazardous contents, hot work, lifting, sharp edges and other serious risks. Practical work must only occur under appropriate workplace isolation, supervision and legal controls.

5. Quality awareness

Correct material identity, dimensions, component type, inspection requirements and traceability are important quality concepts. Learners should understand these controls before entering supervised practical training.
