Reasoning and Problem Solving

Curriculum-aligned learning support for emotional intelligence, reasoning, problem solving and active learning.

← KM-09 lessons
Learning support only. This module develops workplace thinking and interpersonal skills. It does not diagnose personality, mental health or competence, and it does not replace facilitator or workplace judgement.

1. What reasoning is

Reasoning is the process of using information, evidence and logic to reach a conclusion. In technical work, good reasoning helps learners separate facts from assumptions.

2. Different types of reasoning

Reasoning may involve moving from general rules to a specific conclusion, building a general conclusion from repeated observations, or comparing possible explanations. The important habit is to make the thinking process explicit and evidence-based.

3. Problem-solving approaches

A structured problem-solving approach usually starts by defining the problem clearly, gathering relevant information, considering possible causes or options, selecting a suitable response, and reviewing the result.

4. Avoiding premature conclusions

Jumping to a conclusion before checking evidence can create errors. Learners should ask what is known, what is assumed, and what information is still missing.

Key idea: Good problem solving starts with understanding the problem accurately.

5. Escalation

Some problems require input from a supervisor, facilitator or specialist. Knowing when to stop and escalate is part of sound reasoning, especially when safety, quality or authorisation is involved.
