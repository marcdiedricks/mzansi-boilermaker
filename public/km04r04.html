Forming and Shaping Simple Components

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for the South African National Occupational Certificate: Boilermaker.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports recognition, terminology, drawings, specifications, hazards and quality awareness. It does not teach or authorise cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation, lifting or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Terminology, codes and symbols

Forming and shaping work is described through controlled terminology, drawings, symbols, notes and specifications. The learner should be able to recognise this information and understand that the current approved document controls the job.

Key idea: Recognise the information before any physical process is considered.

2. Simple components and steel shapes

Simple components can be made from plate, pipe or structural sections. At knowledge level, learners should distinguish component types and understand that shape, material and intended use affect the applicable requirements.

3. Material identification

Different materials and sections have different properties and limitations. Material identity and suitability must come from approved job information and workplace identification systems, not appearance alone.

4. Hazards and safe work practices

Forming and shaping may involve heavy material, stored energy, powered equipment, heat, sharp edges and moving parts. Learners should recognise these hazard families and know that practical activity requires task-specific controls and supervision.

5. Drawings and specifications

Dimensions, symbols, abbreviations and notes on drawings communicate the required result. If information is missing or conflicting, the correct learning response is to stop and seek clarification rather than improvise.
