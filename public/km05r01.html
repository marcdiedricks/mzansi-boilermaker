Joining of Simple Components

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for Assembling and Joining.

← KM-05 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports terminology, drawings, specifications, hazard recognition and planning. It does not teach or authorise welding, lifting, hoisting, slinging, powered machinery or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Joining terminology and principles

Joining describes methods used to connect components so that the assembly meets the drawing, specification and intended purpose. Learners should understand the language of joints, fasteners, fit and alignment before practical work begins.

2. Drawings, codes and specifications

The correct joint type, location, dimensions and quality requirements must be read from approved information. Similar-looking assemblies are not automatically equivalent.

Key idea: Controlled drawings and specifications remain the authority.

3. Hazards and risks

Joining work can involve hot work, sharp edges, heavy components, pinch points, powered tools and stored energy. Learners should recognise these hazard families and understand that practical work requires task-specific controls.

4. Assembling and fastening awareness

Different assemblies may use different fastening or joining methods. At knowledge level the learner should distinguish method categories and their intended roles without performing the operation.

5. Layout and fit awareness

Correct fit depends on controlled dimensions, alignment and component identity. Any mismatch, unclear drawing or unsuitable component must be escalated rather than forced or improvised.
