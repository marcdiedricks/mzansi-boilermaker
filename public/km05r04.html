Lifting of Loads — Theory and Safety Awareness

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for Assembling and Joining.

← KM-05 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports terminology, drawings, specifications, hazard recognition and planning. It does not teach or authorise welding, lifting, hoisting, slinging, powered machinery or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Lifting as a controlled activity

Lifting loads is a high-risk workplace activity. Knowledge learning should focus on recognising roles, planning controls, load information and the requirement for competent supervision rather than operating lifting equipment.

2. Communication and hand signals

Standardised communication helps people coordinate lifting operations. Learners may study the meaning and purpose of recognised signals in an approved training setting, but signalling real lifting work requires workplace authorisation and supervision.

3. Load charts and working-load information

Load charts and equipment ratings provide controlled limits for specific equipment and configurations. Learners should understand that these figures must be read from the correct current source and cannot be guessed or transferred between equipment.

4. Calculation awareness

The curriculum includes working-load calculations. In the PWA these should remain classroom-style examples checked by a facilitator; they must not be used to plan a real lift.

Key idea: A calculation result is learning evidence, not permission to lift.

5. Safety boundary

Real lifting involves hazards such as suspended loads, crush zones, equipment failure and unstable conditions. Learners must stay outside operational decision-making unless trained, authorised and supervised.
