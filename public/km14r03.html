Lifting Operations Using Manual Equipment — Planning Concepts

Curriculum-aligned theory support for basic slinging, lifting and load securing.

← KM-14 lessons
High-risk theory only. Slinging, lifting, crane direction, load movement and load securing can cause severe injury if done incorrectly. This lesson teaches recognition, planning concepts, hazard awareness and decision boundaries only. It does not provide rigging instructions, sling-selection procedures, working-load calculations, hand-signal sequences, operating steps or authorisation for real lifting work. Practical training requires approved equipment, formal workplace procedures and competent adult supervision.

1. Planning before movement

A lifting operation requires controlled planning that considers the load, route, environment, equipment, people and applicable procedures.

2. Roles and responsibilities

Real lifting work requires clearly assigned authorised roles. Learners should understand that different people may be responsible for planning, operating, signalling, supervision and exclusion control.

3. Environmental conditions

Floor condition, access, obstructions, visibility and other site factors can affect whether a lifting operation is permitted to proceed.

4. Stop-work judgement

If load information, equipment status, communication or site conditions are unclear, the safe decision is to stop and refer the matter to the responsible supervisor or lifting authority.

Key idea: A planned lift is a controlled workplace operation, not a trial-and-error task.
