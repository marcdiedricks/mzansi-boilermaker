Forming and Shaping Complex Components

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for the South African National Occupational Certificate: Boilermaker.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports recognition, terminology, drawings, specifications, hazards and quality awareness. It does not teach or authorise cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation, lifting or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Complex components

Complex components may involve several surfaces, transitions, intersections, offsets or connected features. Learners should recognise the component type and understand how drawings and specifications describe the intended result.

2. Drawings and specifications

Complex work often depends on multiple views, dimensions, notes, tolerances and reference points. The learner should check that the correct drawing and revision are being used before interpreting the information.

Key idea: Complexity increases the importance of controlled information.

3. Materials and structural shapes

Plate, pipe and structural sections can behave differently and may be subject to different project requirements. The material and section must be identified from approved documentation.

4. Hazard awareness

Complex forming may involve heavy material, stored energy, powered machinery, heat or other high-risk conditions. The learner should identify hazard categories and understand that practical methods must be taught and supervised in approved environments.

5. Quality awareness

A complex component must ultimately be checked against the required geometry, dimensions, tolerances and specifications by the appropriate training or workplace process. The PWA supports understanding but does not approve work.
