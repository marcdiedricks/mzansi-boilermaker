Basic Principles of Design Thinking for Innovation

Curriculum-aligned learning support for basic computers, communication software, digital responsibility, 4IR and design thinking.

Learning support only. This module develops basic digital literacy for the occupational environment. Workplace IT policies, privacy rules, cybersecurity controls and authorised software usage remain binding.

1. What design thinking is

Design thinking is a structured way of understanding a problem, considering the people affected, generating ideas and improving a proposed solution through feedback and testing.

2. Useful attributes

Helpful attributes include curiosity, empathy, openness to different ideas, willingness to learn from feedback and attention to the real problem rather than jumping to a solution.

3. Core principles

A strong design-thinking process keeps the user or worker need visible, works with evidence, explores alternatives and improves ideas through small tests.

4. Skills and techniques

Useful techniques can include observation, asking questions, defining the problem clearly, sketching ideas, comparing alternatives and reviewing feedback.

5. Innovation in trade environments

Innovation does not always mean advanced technology. A clearer workflow, safer information handoff or better learning aid can also be useful innovation.

Key idea: Understand first, build second.
