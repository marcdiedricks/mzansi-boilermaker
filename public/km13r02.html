Self-Governance and Decision Making

Curriculum-aligned learning support for decision making, self-governance, integrity and personal responsibility.

← KM-13 lessons
Learning support only. These lessons develop thinking, ethics and workplace judgement. They do not replace workplace authority, legal duties, supervision or formal assessment.

1. What self-governance means

Self-governance means managing one's own choices and behaviour responsibly rather than relying only on external pressure.

2. Components of self-governance

Useful components include self-awareness, discipline, reflection, accountability and the ability to consider consequences before acting.

3. Motivation and consequences

Decisions can be influenced by internal motivation, external pressure and expected consequences. Learners should notice these influences instead of pretending they do not exist.

4. Expert input

Expert advice can improve a decision, but the learner should still understand the basis of the advice, its limits and the responsibility that remains with the authorised decision-maker.

5. Importance of self-governance

Self-governance and integrity support responsible participation in workplaces and communities.

Key idea: Good judgement includes listening to expertise without surrendering responsibility for your own choices.
