Joining of Steel Structures

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for Assembling and Joining.

← KM-05 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports terminology, drawings, specifications, hazard recognition and planning. It does not teach or authorise welding, lifting, hoisting, slinging, powered machinery or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Types of steel structures

Steel structures can contain different members, connections and load paths. Learners should recognise common structural arrangements and understand that joining requirements depend on the approved design.

2. Joining method categories

Different structural connections may use different joining approaches. The learner should understand advantages, limitations and quality implications in principle, while practical execution remains supervised.

3. Strengthening of joints

Structural joints may include designed features that improve strength or stiffness. These details must come from drawings, engineering requirements and approved specifications rather than improvised changes.

4. Jigs and fixtures

Jigs and fixtures can help control position, repeatability and alignment. Their use must be appropriate to the job and workplace method, and their presence does not replace inspection or supervision.

5. Drawings and specifications

Structural assembly depends on correct views, dimensions, notes and revision status. Learners should practise interpreting this information and identifying where clarification is required.
