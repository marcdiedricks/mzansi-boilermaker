Recognise the Controlled Fabrication Sequence Before Work Begins

A short recognition-and-planning lesson within KM-04 Fabrication and Pipework.

← Main app

Learning only. This lesson explains why a controlled work sequence matters before fabrication. It does not teach cutting, welding, heating, bending, lifting, machine setup, machine operation or other hazardous practical procedures. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. What is a fabrication sequence?

A fabrication sequence is the controlled order in which planned stages of a job are intended to take place. The required sequence must come from approved job information, drawings, specifications, procedures or responsible workplace instruction.

2. Sequence affects the whole job

The order of work can affect dimensions, alignment, access, inspection and later stages. A learner should therefore recognise the planned sequence before practical work begins.

3. Do not invent or rearrange the sequence

If the required order is missing, unclear or conflicts with controlled information, stop and clarify it through the responsible training or workplace process. Do not guess or change the sequence independently.

4. Recognition is not authorisation

Understanding the planned sequence does not authorise a learner to perform fabrication. Practical application belongs in approved practical training under appropriate workplace controls and supervision.
