Moving Loads with Rollers and Sliders — Theory and Hazard Awareness

Curriculum-aligned theory support for basic slinging, lifting and load securing.

← KM-14 lessons
High-risk theory only. Slinging, lifting, crane direction, load movement and load securing can cause severe injury if done incorrectly. This lesson teaches recognition, planning concepts, hazard awareness and decision boundaries only. It does not provide rigging instructions, sling-selection procedures, working-load calculations, hand-signal sequences, operating steps or authorisation for real lifting work. Practical training requires approved equipment, formal workplace procedures and competent adult supervision.

1. Purpose of rollers and sliders

Rollers and sliders are used in some controlled workplaces to assist with moving heavy loads over short distances. Learners should understand the concept without attempting the activity.

2. Movement hazards

Heavy loads can shift unexpectedly, trap people, damage surfaces or become unstable. Pinch and crush zones are a major concern in any load-moving activity.

3. Planning and route control

A real move requires an approved plan, suitable route, authorised equipment, controlled access and competent supervision.

4. Stop and escalate

If the load, path, surface, equipment or supervision is not clearly controlled, learners should not attempt the move.

Key idea: No person should enter a crush or pinch zone to improvise a load movement.
