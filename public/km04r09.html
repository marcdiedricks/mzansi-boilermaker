Marking Off Pipe Work

Curriculum-aligned knowledge support for the South African National Occupational Certificate: Boilermaker.

← KM-04 lessons
Knowledge learning only. This lesson supports recognition, terminology, drawings, specifications, hazards and quality awareness. It does not teach or authorise cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine operation, lifting or other hazardous practical work. Practical activities require approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Purpose of marking-off information

Marking-off translates approved drawing and dimensional information into reference information for later supervised fabrication. At knowledge level, learners should understand its purpose and the need for accuracy without carrying out hazardous fabrication activity.

2. Contour marker awareness

A contour marker is a specialised layout aid used in some pipework contexts. Learners may identify its purpose and main parts in a controlled learning environment, but practical setup and use must be taught by a qualified facilitator using approved procedures.

3. Centre finder awareness

A centre finder is used to help establish reference information on cylindrical work. Knowledge learning should focus on what the tool is for and why correct identification and condition matter, not on unsupervised operating instructions.

4. Cutting-angle calculations

The curriculum expects understanding of calculations associated with pipe layout. In this companion, calculation practice should use classroom-style examples supplied or checked by a facilitator; it must not become an instruction to cut or modify real pipework.

Key idea: A correct calculation is not practical authorisation.

5. Care, inspection and escalation

Layout tools should be treated as controlled equipment. If a tool, drawing or dimensional requirement is unclear or damaged, learners should stop and refer the matter to the responsible facilitator or supervisor.
