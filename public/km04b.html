Read the Job Information Before Fabrication

A short planning-and-recognition lesson within KM-04 Fabrication and Pipework.

← Main app
Learning only. This lesson explains how controlled job information guides planning. It does not teach cutting, welding, heating, bending, machine setup or machine operation. Practical work requires approved training, workplace controls and competent human supervision.

1. Start with controlled job information

Before fabrication begins, the learner must understand what the job requires. Controlled job information can include an approved drawing, dimensions, material requirements, specifications, notes and workplace instructions.

Key idea: Do not begin from memory or guesswork when controlled job information is available.

2. Read the drawing as a communication tool

A drawing communicates what must be made and how parts relate to one another. Important information can include views, dimensions, notes, identifiers and revision information.

This lesson is about recognising and checking that information, not performing fabrication operations.

3. Dimensions and tolerances matter

Dimensions state required sizes or positions. A tolerance describes the permitted variation from a stated requirement where one is specified.

The learner should never invent a dimension or assume that an approximate size is acceptable when the controlled job information gives a requirement.

4. Match the material to the requirement

Material type, section, plate, pipe, size and other specified characteristics can affect the whole job. Material suitability must be confirmed from controlled information and approved identification methods, not appearance alone.

5. Think about sequence before action

Planning means understanding the required result, the information available, the order in which the job will be approached, and the checks that must happen before practical activity begins.

Planning rule: Understand first. Clarify uncertainty. Practical execution comes later under the correct workplace controls.

6. Stop-and-ask rule

Stop and ask a responsible supervisor or training professional when a drawing, revision, dimension, material requirement, note or work instruction is unclear or appears inconsistent.

Clarifying controlled information is part of safe and accurate work preparation.
